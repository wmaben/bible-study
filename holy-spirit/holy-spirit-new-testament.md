# HOLY SPIRIT IN THE NEW TESTAMENT

---

## Part 1 — The Gospels

## 1. Matthew

### Matthew 1:18, 20

> **18** “Now the birth of Jesus Christ was on this wise: When as his mother Mary was espoused to Joseph, before they came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost.  
> **20** But while he thought on these things, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto him in a dream, saying, Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** Matthew explains that Jesus’ conception was the miraculous work of the Holy Spirit, not the result of a human father. The angel reassured Joseph and directed him to receive Mary as his wife.

---

### Matthew 3:11, 16

> **11** “I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance: but he that cometh after me is mightier than I, whose shoes I am not worthy to bear: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire:  
> **16** And Jesus, when he was baptized, went up straightway out of the water: and, lo, the heavens were opened unto him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove, and lighting upon him:”

**Context:** John contrasted his baptism of repentance with the greater work of the coming Messiah. At Jesus’ baptism, the Spirit descended upon Him while the Father declared His approval, publicly marking the beginning of Jesus’ ministry.

---

### Matthew 4:1

> “Then was Jesus led up of the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted of the devil.”

**Context:** Immediately after His baptism, Jesus was led by the Spirit into the wilderness. His temptation was not an accident; it occurred within God’s purpose as Jesus faithfully resisted Satan.

---

### Matthew 10:20

> “For it is not ye that speak, but the Spirit of your Father which speaketh in you.”

**Context:** Jesus prepared the Twelve for persecution as they proclaimed the kingdom. When brought before rulers, they were to depend upon the Father’s Spirit for the words needed to bear witness.

---

### Matthew 12:18, 28, 31–32

> **18** “Behold my servant, whom I have chosen; my beloved, in whom my soul is well pleased: I will put my spirit upon him, and he shall shew judgment to the Gentiles.  
> **28** But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you.  
> **31** Wherefore I say unto you, All manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men: but the blasphemy against the Holy Ghost shall not be forgiven unto men.  
> **32** And whosoever speaketh a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but whosoever speaketh against the Holy Ghost, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, neither in the world to come.”

**Context:** Matthew applies Isaiah’s Servant prophecy to Jesus, whose ministry is empowered by the Spirit. After Jesus cast out a demon, the Pharisees attributed His work to Satan. Jesus warned that persistently and knowingly identifying the Spirit’s evident work as demonic reveals hardened opposition to the very One who brings conviction and forgiveness.

---

### Matthew 22:43

> “He saith unto them, How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying,”

**Context:** Jesus questioned the Pharisees about Psalm 110 and the identity of the Messiah. David’s inspired words show that the Messiah is not merely David’s descendant but also David’s Lord; Mark 12:36 explicitly says that David spoke this by the Holy Ghost.

---

### Matthew 28:19

> “Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost:”

**Context:** In the Great Commission, the risen Jesus commands His disciples to make disciples among all nations. Baptism is administered in the one “name” of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost.

---

## 2. Mark

### Mark 1:8, 10, 12

> **8** “I indeed have baptized you with water: but he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost.  
> **10** And straightway coming up out of the water, he saw the heavens opened, and the Spirit like a dove descending upon him:  
> **12** And immediately the Spirit driveth him into the wilderness.”

**Context:** John announced that Jesus would give the Holy Spirit. The Spirit descended upon Jesus at His baptism and immediately directed Him into the wilderness, connecting His anointing with His obedient confrontation of temptation.

---

### Mark 3:29

> “But he that shall blaspheme against the Holy Ghost hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation:”

**Context:** Religious leaders said that Jesus expelled demons by the ruler of demons. Jesus’ warning concerns their deliberate rejection and slander of the Holy Spirit’s manifest work through Him, not an accidental statement by someone who desires repentance.

---

### Mark 12:36

> “For David himself said by the Holy Ghost, The Lord said to my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool.”

**Context:** Jesus cites Psalm 110 to show that the promised Christ is greater than David. He also identifies the Holy Ghost as the divine source of David’s prophetic Scripture.

---

### Mark 13:11

> “But when they shall lead you, and deliver you up, take no thought beforehand what ye shall speak, neither do ye premeditate: but whatsoever shall be given you in that hour, that speak ye: for it is not ye that speak, but the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** Jesus warned His followers that they would face arrest and opposition. In those moments, the Holy Ghost would enable their faithful testimony; this promise concerns Spirit-given witness under persecution rather than a rejection of ordinary preparation for teaching.

---

## 3. Luke

### Luke 1:15, 35, 41, 67

> **15** “For he shall be great in the sight of the Lord, and shall drink neither wine nor strong drink; and he shall be filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother’s womb.  
> **35** And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.  
> **41** And it came to pass, that, when Elisabeth heard the salutation of Mary, the babe leaped in her womb; and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost:  
> **67** And his father Zacharias was filled with the Holy Ghost, and prophesied, saying,”

**Context:** Luke emphasizes the Spirit’s activity before Jesus’ birth. John was set apart for a Spirit-filled prophetic ministry, Jesus was conceived through the Spirit’s miraculous power, and both Elisabeth and Zacharias were enabled to speak prophetically about God’s saving work.

---

### Luke 2:25–27

> **25** “And, behold, there was a man in Jerusalem, whose name was Simeon; and the same man was just and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel: and the Holy Ghost was upon him.  
> **26** And it was revealed unto him by the Holy Ghost, that he should not see death, before he had seen the Lord’s Christ.  
> **27** And he came by the Spirit into the temple: and when the parents brought in the child Jesus, to do for him after the custom of the law,”

**Context:** Simeon waited faithfully for Israel’s promised consolation. The Spirit rested upon him, revealed that he would see the Messiah, and guided him into the Temple when Mary and Joseph brought Jesus there.

---

### Luke 3:16, 22

> **16** “John answered, saying unto them all, I indeed baptize you with water; but one mightier than I cometh, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to unloose: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire:  
> **22** And the Holy Ghost descended in a bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven, which said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased.”

**Context:** John pointed beyond his water baptism to the Messiah’s greater baptism with the Holy Ghost and fire. At Jesus’ baptism, the Spirit visibly descended and the Father identified Jesus as His beloved Son.

---

### Luke 4:1, 14, 18

> **1** “And Jesus being full of the Holy Ghost returned from Jordan, and was led by the Spirit into the wilderness,  
> **14** And Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit into Galilee: and there went out a fame of him through all the region round about.  
> **18** The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised,”

**Context:** Jesus entered temptation full of and led by the Spirit, then began His Galilean ministry in the Spirit’s power. In Nazareth He read Isaiah 61 and declared Himself the anointed bearer of good news, freedom, healing, and God’s favor.

---

### Luke 10:21

> “In that hour Jesus rejoiced in spirit, and said, I thank thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes: even so, Father; for so it seemed good in thy sight.”

**Context:** After the seventy returned, Jesus rejoiced over God’s revelation to humble disciples. The KJV has “rejoiced in spirit”; many modern translations understand the phrase as rejoicing **in the Holy Spirit**, while the wording can also be read as describing Jesus’ own spirit.

---

### Luke 11:13

> “If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?”

**Context:** Jesus concludes His teaching on persistent prayer by presenting the Holy Spirit as the Father’s supreme good gift. God is more willing to give what is truly good than even an earthly parent.

---

### Luke 12:10, 12

> **10** “And whosoever shall speak a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him: but unto him that blasphemeth against the Holy Ghost it shall not be forgiven.  
> **12** For the Holy Ghost shall teach you in the same hour what ye ought to say.”

**Context:** Jesus combines a warning against hardened rejection of the Holy Ghost with encouragement for persecuted disciples. The same Spirit who must not be slandered will teach Christ’s witnesses what to say when they are brought before authorities.

---

### Luke 24:49 — The Promise of the Father

> “And, behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you: but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high.”

**Context:** This is an indirect reference to the Holy Spirit. The risen Jesus told the disciples to wait in Jerusalem for the Father’s promised power; Acts 1:4–5 identifies that promise with baptism in the Holy Ghost, fulfilled at Pentecost.

---

## 4. John

### John 1:32–33

> **32** “And John bare record, saying, I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, and it abode upon him.  
> **33** And I knew him not: but he that sent me to baptize with water, the same said unto me, Upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending, and remaining on him, the same is he which baptizeth with the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** John the Baptist identifies Jesus by the divinely given sign of the Spirit descending and remaining upon Him. Jesus is not only Spirit-anointed; He is the One who baptizes others with the Holy Ghost.

---

### John 3:5–6, 8, 34

> **5** “Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.  
> **6** That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.  
> **8** The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.  
> **34** For he whom God hath sent speaketh the words of God: for God giveth not the Spirit by measure unto him.”

**Context:** Jesus tells Nicodemus that entrance into God’s kingdom requires spiritual birth produced by the Spirit, whose sovereign work is compared to the unseen yet observable wind. Later, the Gospel explains that God’s sent One speaks God’s words and possesses the Spirit without measure.

---

### John 6:63

> “It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.”

**Context:** After many struggled with Jesus’ teaching about the bread of life, He explained that life is imparted spiritually rather than by merely physical understanding. “The spirit” is commonly understood as the Holy Spirit, though the statement also contrasts spiritual reality with unaided flesh.

---

### John 7:39

> “(But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive: for the Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified.)”

**Context:** Jesus had promised rivers of living water to those who believed in Him. John explains that the water signified the Spirit, whose new-covenant outpouring would follow Jesus’ death, resurrection, and glorification.

---

### John 14:16–17, 26

> **16** “And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever;  
> **17** Even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you.  
> **26** But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you.”

**Context:** On the night before His crucifixion, Jesus promised that His disciples would not be abandoned. The Father would send “another Comforter”—the Holy Ghost and Spirit of truth—to remain with them, dwell in them, teach them, and bring Jesus’ words to their remembrance.

---

### John 15:26

> “But when the Comforter is come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth, which proceedeth from the Father, he shall testify of me:”

**Context:** Jesus describes the Spirit as proceeding from the Father and being sent to bear witness concerning the Son. The disciples would also testify, but their witness would be sustained by the Spirit’s prior testimony.

---

### John 16:7–15

> **7** “Nevertheless I tell you the truth; It is expedient for you that I go away: for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you; but if I depart, I will send him unto you.  
> **8** And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:  
> **9** Of sin, because they believe not on me;  
> **10** Of righteousness, because I go to my Father, and ye see me no more;  
> **11** Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged.  
> **12** I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.  
> **13** Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth: for he shall not speak of himself; but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak: and he will shew you things to come.  
> **14** He shall glorify me: for he shall receive of mine, and shall shew it unto you.  
> **15** All things that the Father hath are mine: therefore said I, that he shall take of mine, and shall shew it unto you.”

**Context:** Jesus says that His departure will lead to the Comforter’s coming. The Spirit will convict the world, guide the apostles into truth, disclose what He receives, and glorify Christ rather than draw attention away from Him.

### The titles in John 14–16

John calls the Holy Spirit the **Comforter** and the **Spirit of truth**. “Comforter” translates a term that can also convey helper, advocate, counselor, or one called alongside. These chapters present the Spirit as personal—He teaches, testifies, hears, speaks, guides, and glorifies Christ.

---

### John 20:22

> “And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost:”

**Context:** On the evening of the resurrection, Jesus commissioned His disciples and breathed on them. Interpreters differ on whether this was an immediate impartation or a symbolic and anticipatory act pointing to Pentecost, but the scene deliberately joins the risen Christ, new life, mission, and the Holy Ghost.

## Part 2 — Acts of the Apostles

Acts presents the Holy Spirit as the promised gift of the Father who empowers witness, forms the church, directs its mission, confirms the inclusion of new peoples, appoints leaders, and speaks through Scripture and prophecy.

### Acts 1:2, 4–5, 8, 16

> **2** “Until the day in which he was taken up, after that he through the Holy Ghost had given commandments unto the apostles whom he had chosen:  
> **4** And, being assembled together with them, commanded them that they should not depart from Jerusalem, but wait for the promise of the Father, which, saith he, ye have heard of me.  
> **5** For John truly baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence.  
> **8** But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.  
> **16** Men and brethren, this scripture must needs have been fulfilled, which the Holy Ghost by the mouth of David spake before concerning Judas, which was guide to them that took Jesus.”

**Context:** Before His ascension, Jesus ordered the apostles to wait for the Father’s promise—the baptism and empowering of the Holy Ghost. Their Spirit-enabled witness would advance outward from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth. Peter also affirmed that the Spirit had spoken prophetically through David concerning Judas.

---

### Acts 2:4, 17–18, 33, 38

> **4** “And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.  
> **17** And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams:  
> **18** And on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit; and they shall prophesy:  
> **33** Therefore being by the right hand of God exalted, and having received of the Father the promise of the Holy Ghost, he hath shed forth this, which ye now see and hear.  
> **38** Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** At Pentecost, the waiting disciples were filled with the Spirit and spoke in other languages. Peter interpreted the event through Joel’s promise of an outpouring upon all kinds of people and explained that the exalted Jesus had poured out the promised Spirit. He then called his hearers to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift.

---

### Acts 4:8, 31

> **8** “Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, said unto them, Ye rulers of the people, and elders of Israel,  
> **31** And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.”

**Context:** After Peter and John were arrested for preaching Jesus’ resurrection, the Spirit enabled Peter to answer Israel’s rulers boldly. When the believers later prayed—not for safety, but for courage—they were freshly filled and continued speaking God’s word with boldness.

---

### Acts 5:3, 9, 32

> **3** “But Peter said, Ananias, why hath Satan filled thine heart to lie to the Holy Ghost, and to keep back part of the price of the land?  
> **9** Then Peter said unto her, How is it that ye have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? behold, the feet of them which have buried thy husband are at the door, and shall carry thee out.  
> **32** And we are his witnesses of these things; and so is also the Holy Ghost, whom God hath given to them that obey him.”

**Context:** Ananias and Sapphira falsely represented their gift while seeking the appearance of complete generosity. Peter describes their deception as lying to the Holy Ghost and testing the Spirit of the Lord. Later, before the council, the apostles identify the Spirit as God’s co-witness to Jesus’ exaltation.

---

### Acts 6:3, 5, 10

> **3** “Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.  
> **5** And the saying pleased the whole multitude: and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, and Philip, and Prochorus, and Nicanor, and Timon, and Parmenas, and Nicolas a proselyte of Antioch:  
> **10** And they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake.”

**Context:** The Jerusalem church selected Spirit-filled, wise men to oversee the fair distribution of food. Stephen’s fullness of the Spirit was evident not only in service and character but also in wisdom that his opponents could not overcome.

---

### Acts 7:51, 55

> **51** “Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye.  
> **55** But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God,”

**Context:** At the end of his defense, Stephen accused the council of resisting the Holy Ghost as their ancestors had resisted the prophets. Full of that same Spirit, Stephen was given a vision of God’s glory and the exalted Jesus before he was martyred.

---

### Acts 8:15–19, 29, 39

> **15** “Who, when they were come down, prayed for them, that they might receive the Holy Ghost:  
> **16** (For as yet he was fallen upon none of them: only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.)  
> **17** Then laid they their hands on them, and they received the Holy Ghost.  
> **18** And when Simon saw that through laying on of the apostles’ hands the Holy Ghost was given, he offered them money,  
> **19** Saying, Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I lay hands, he may receive the Holy Ghost.  
> **29** Then the Spirit said unto Philip, Go near, and join thyself to this chariot.  
> **39** And when they were come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip, that the eunuch saw him no more: and he went on his way rejoicing.”

**Context:** Peter and John prayed for Samaritan believers, and their reception of the Spirit visibly confirmed that they belonged to the one church. Simon’s attempt to buy spiritual authority was sharply condemned. The Spirit then directed Philip to an Ethiopian official and removed him after the man believed and was baptized.

---

### Acts 9:17, 31

> **17** “And Ananias went his way, and entered into the house; and putting his hands on him said, Brother Saul, the Lord, even Jesus, that appeared unto thee in the way as thou camest, hath sent me, that thou mightest receive thy sight, and be filled with the Holy Ghost.  
> **31** Then had the churches rest throughout all Judaea and Galilee and Samaria, and were edified; and walking in the fear of the Lord, and in the comfort of the Holy Ghost, were multiplied.”

**Context:** Jesus sent Ananias to the newly converted Saul so that he would regain sight and be filled with the Spirit. The narrative then summarizes the Spirit’s comforting work as the churches were strengthened and multiplied.

---

### Acts 10:19–20, 38, 44–45, 47

> **19** “While Peter thought on the vision, the Spirit said unto him, Behold, three men seek thee.  
> **20** Arise therefore, and get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them.  
> **38** How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil; for God was with him.  
> **44** While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word.  
> **45** And they of the circumcision which believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost.  
> **47** Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?”

**Context:** The Spirit directed Peter to the Gentile centurion Cornelius, overcoming Peter’s hesitation. As Peter preached the Spirit-anointed ministry, death, and resurrection of Jesus, the Spirit fell upon the Gentile hearers. This divine gift proved that they could be baptized and received without becoming Jewish proselytes first.

---

### Acts 11:12, 15–16, 24, 28

> **12** “And the spirit bade me go with them, nothing doubting. Moreover these six brethren accompanied me, and we entered into the man’s house:  
> **15** And as I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning.  
> **16** Then remembered I the word of the Lord, how that he said, John indeed baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost.  
> **24** For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith: and much people was added unto the Lord.  
> **28** And there stood up one of them named Agabus, and signified by the spirit that there should be great dearth throughout all the world: which came to pass in the days of Claudius Caesar.”

**Context:** Peter defended his fellowship with Gentiles by recounting the Spirit’s direction and the Gentiles’ Pentecost-like reception of the Holy Ghost. Luke then describes Barnabas as Spirit-filled and records Agabus’s Spirit-given warning of famine, which prompted practical relief.

---

### Acts 13:2, 4, 9, 52

> **2** “As they ministered to the Lord, and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them.  
> **4** So they, being sent forth by the Holy Ghost, departed unto Seleucia; and from thence they sailed to Cyprus.  
> **9** Then Saul, (who also is called Paul,) filled with the Holy Ghost, set his eyes on him,  
> **52** And the disciples were filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** During worship and fasting at Antioch, the Holy Ghost personally called and sent Barnabas and Saul for missionary work. The Spirit empowered Paul to confront Elymas, and He filled new disciples with joy even amid opposition.

---

### Acts 15:8, 28

> **8** “And God, which knoweth the hearts, bare them witness, giving them the Holy Ghost, even as he did unto us;  
> **28** For it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things;”

**Context:** At the Jerusalem council, Peter appealed to God’s gift of the Spirit to uncircumcised Gentiles as proof of their acceptance by faith. The council’s final letter expressed its decision as a conclusion reached under the Holy Ghost’s guidance.

---

### Acts 16:6–7

> **6** “Now when they had gone throughout Phrygia and the region of Galatia, and were forbidden of the Holy Ghost to preach the word in Asia,  
> **7** After they were come to Mysia, they assayed to go into Bithynia: but the Spirit suffered them not.”

**Context:** The missionary team’s route was not determined by strategy alone. The Spirit closed certain paths before directing Paul toward Macedonia, demonstrating His active governance of the church’s mission.

---

### Acts 19:2, 6

> **2** “He said unto them, Have ye received the Holy Ghost since ye believed? And they said unto him, We have not so much as heard whether there be any Holy Ghost.  
> **6** And when Paul had laid his hands upon them, the Holy Ghost came on them; and they spake with tongues, and prophesied.”

**Context:** At Ephesus, Paul met disciples who knew only John’s baptism. After hearing about Jesus, they were baptized in His name; when Paul laid hands on them, the Spirit came upon them with visible prophetic signs.

---

### Acts 20:23, 28

> **23** “Save that the Holy Ghost witnesseth in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions abide me.  
> **28** Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock, over the which the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood.”

**Context:** Paul knew through repeated Spirit-given warnings that suffering awaited him in Jerusalem. Addressing the Ephesian elders, he said that the Holy Ghost had appointed them to watch over and shepherd God’s church.

---

### Acts 21:4, 11

> **4** “And finding disciples, we tarried there seven days: who said to Paul through the Spirit, that he should not go up to Jerusalem.  
> **11** And when he was come unto us, he took Paul’s girdle, and bound his own hands and feet, and said, Thus saith the Holy Ghost, So shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man that owneth this girdle, and shall deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles.”

**Context:** Spirit-given warnings accompanied Paul’s journey. Agabus dramatically foretold Paul’s arrest; interpreters commonly distinguish the Spirit’s revelation of danger from the disciples’ conclusion that Paul should avoid Jerusalem, since Paul remained convinced that he must go.

---

### Acts 28:25

> “And when they agreed not among themselves, they departed, after that Paul had spoken one word, Well spake the Holy Ghost by Esaias the prophet unto our fathers,”

**Context:** In Rome, some Jewish hearers believed Paul’s message and others rejected it. Paul introduced Isaiah’s warning about hardened hearing as words spoken by the Holy Ghost, again affirming the Spirit’s authorship of prophetic Scripture.

### Translation and interpretation notes in Acts

#### Acts 4:25

> “Who by the mouth of thy servant David hast said, Why did the heathen rage, and the people imagine vain things?”

The KJV does not explicitly mention the Holy Ghost in this verse. Several modern translations follow wording such as “who by the Holy Spirit, through the mouth of our father David,” making explicit that the gathered believers regarded Psalm 2 as Spirit-inspired.

#### Acts 16:7

The KJV says **“the Spirit suffered them not.”** Many modern translations read **“the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them,”** reflecting a textual form that supplies the title “of Jesus.” In either wording, the Spirit directs the mission.

#### Acts 19:21 and 20:22

> **Acts 19:21** “After these things were ended, Paul purposed in the spirit, when he had passed through Macedonia and Achaia, to go to Jerusalem, saying, After I have been there, I must also see Rome.  
> **Acts 20:22** And now, behold, I go bound in the spirit unto Jerusalem, not knowing the things that shall befall me there:”

These statements may describe direction by the Holy Spirit, but they may instead refer to Paul’s own inward resolve or sense of compulsion. For that reason, they should not be treated as unambiguous direct references.

## Part 3 — Paul’s Letters

Paul describes the Holy Spirit as God’s gift through Christ, the One who gives life, reveals truth, assures believers of adoption, produces holiness, distributes gifts, creates the church’s unity, and guarantees the completion of salvation.

## 1. Romans

### Romans 1:4

> “And declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead:”

**Context:** Paul opens Romans by summarizing the gospel concerning God’s Son, descended from David and powerfully designated as Son through resurrection. “The spirit of holiness” is commonly understood as a title for the Holy Spirit, though some interpret it as describing Christ’s holy spiritual mode of existence in contrast with His Davidic descent “according to the flesh.”

---

### Romans 5:5

> “And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.”

**Context:** Because believers have been justified by faith, suffering can produce endurance and hope rather than final shame. The indwelling Holy Ghost gives inward assurance by pouring God’s love into their hearts.

---

### Romans 8:2, 4–6, 9–16, 23, 26–27

> **2** “For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus hath made me free from the law of sin and death.  
> **4** That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.  
> **5** For they that are after the flesh do mind the things of the flesh; but they that are after the Spirit the things of the Spirit.  
> **6** For to be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.

> **12** Therefore, brethren, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live after the flesh.

**Context:** Romans 8 contrasts life in the flesh with new life in the Spirit. The indwelling Spirit frees believers from sin’s rule, empowers holiness, confirms their adoption, and guarantees bodily resurrection. As believers await full redemption, the Spirit helps their weakness and intercedes according to God’s will.

Romans 8:9 uses **“Spirit of God”** and **“Spirit of Christ”** for the same indwelling Spirit. Paul thereby closely relates the Spirit’s presence to belonging to Christ.

### Romans 9:1

> “I say the truth in Christ, I lie not, my conscience also bearing me witness in the Holy Ghost,”

**Context:** Paul introduces his anguish over Israel’s unbelief with a solemn declaration. His conscience, illuminated and confirmed by the Holy Ghost, bears witness that his grief is genuine.

---

### Romans 14:17

> “For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink; but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** In disputes over food and observance of days, believers must not destroy fellowship over secondary matters. God’s reign is displayed not by dietary practice but by Spirit-produced righteousness, peace, and joy.

### Romans 15:13, 16, 19, 30

> **13** “Now the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that ye may abound in hope, through the power of the Holy Ghost.  
> **16** That I should be the minister of Jesus Christ to the Gentiles, ministering the gospel of God, that the offering up of the Gentiles might be acceptable, being sanctified by the Holy Ghost.  
> **19** Through mighty signs and wonders, by the power of the Spirit of God; so that from Jerusalem, and round about unto Illyricum, I have fully preached the gospel of Christ.  
> **30** Now I beseech you, brethren, for the Lord Jesus Christ’s sake, and for the love of the Spirit, that ye strive together with me in your prayers to God for me;”

**Context:** Near the letter’s conclusion, Paul attributes Christian hope, the sanctification of Gentile believers, and signs accompanying his mission to the Spirit’s power. He also appeals to the Spirit’s love as he asks the Roman church to join his struggle through prayer.

## 2. First Corinthians

### 1 Corinthians 2:4, 10–14

> **4** “And my speech and my preaching was not with enticing words of man’s wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power:  
> **10** But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit: for the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God.  
> **11** For what man knoweth the things of a man, save the spirit of man which is in him? even so the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God.  
> **12** Now we have received, not the spirit of the world, but the spirit which is of God; that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God.  
> **13** Which things also we speak, not in the words which man’s wisdom teacheth, but which the Holy Ghost teacheth; comparing spiritual things with spiritual.  
> **14** But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him: neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned.”

**Context:** Paul rejected self-exalting rhetoric and depended upon the Spirit’s power. God’s saving wisdom cannot be discovered by unaided human wisdom; the Spirit knows God’s depths, reveals His gifts, teaches the apostolic message, and enables spiritual understanding.

---

### 1 Corinthians 3:16

> “Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you?”

**Context:** Addressing division in the Corinthian church, Paul speaks of the congregation collectively as God’s Temple. The community is holy because God’s Spirit dwells among and within His people.

---

### 1 Corinthians 6:11, 19

> **11** “And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.  
> **19** What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?”

**Context:** The Corinthians’ former identities and practices did not define them after God washed, sanctified, and justified them through Christ and the Spirit. Because the believer’s body is a Temple inhabited by the Holy Ghost and belongs to God, bodily conduct must honor Him.

---

### 1 Corinthians 7:40

> “But she is happier if she so abide, after my judgment: and I think also that I have the Spirit of God.”

**Context:** Concluding counsel about whether a widow should remarry, Paul recommends remaining unmarried while permitting marriage in the Lord. His closing statement affirms that his considered apostolic judgment is not spiritually inferior to competing claims.

---

### 1 Corinthians 12:3–4, 7–9, 11, 13

> **3** “Wherefore I give you to understand, that no man speaking by the Spirit of God calleth Jesus accursed: and that no man can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost.  
> **4** Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit.  
> **7** But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.  
> **8** For to one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit;  
> **9** To another faith by the same Spirit; to another the gifts of healing by the same Spirit;  
> **11** But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will.  
> **13** For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.”

**Context:** Genuine spiritual activity honors Jesus as Lord. Although gifts differ, the one sovereign Spirit distributes them for the common good rather than personal status. The same Spirit incorporates believers of every background into Christ’s one body.

## 3. Second Corinthians

### 2 Corinthians 1:22

> “Who hath also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts.”

**Context:** God establishes believers in Christ, anoints and seals them, and gives the Spirit as an “earnest”—a down payment or guarantee that He will complete His promised salvation.

---

### 2 Corinthians 3:3, 6, 8, 17–18

> **3** “Forasmuch as ye are manifestly declared to be the epistle of Christ ministered by us, written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.  
> **6** Who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.  
> **8** How shall not the ministration of the spirit be rather glorious?  
> **17** Now the Lord is that Spirit: and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.  
> **18** But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.”

**Context:** Paul contrasts the old-covenant ministry engraved on stone with the new-covenant ministry of the Spirit, who writes upon hearts and gives life. Turning to the Lord removes the veil, and the Spirit brings freedom and progressively transforms believers into the Lord’s image.

The statement **“the Lord is that Spirit”** occurs within Paul’s close association of Christ, the Lord, and the Spirit’s new-covenant work. It does not erase their personal distinction, which Paul clearly maintains elsewhere.

---

### 2 Corinthians 5:5

> “Now he that hath wrought us for the selfsame thing is God, who also hath given unto us the earnest of the Spirit.”

**Context:** As believers long for resurrection life, God prepares them for that destiny. The present gift of the Spirit is His pledge that mortality will ultimately be swallowed up by life.

---

### 2 Corinthians 6:6

> “By pureness, by knowledge, by longsuffering, by kindness, by the Holy Ghost, by love unfeigned,”

**Context:** Paul lists the qualities and divine resources that authenticate his ministry amid severe affliction. The Holy Ghost stands alongside purity, patience, kindness, and sincere love as evidence of faithful service.

---

### 2 Corinthians 13:14

> “The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all. Amen.”

**Context:** Paul closes with a blessing that names the Lord Jesus Christ, God, and the Holy Ghost together. Believers live by Christ’s grace, the Father’s love, and fellowship created and shared through the Spirit.

## 4. Galatians

### Galatians 3:2–3, 5, 14

> **2** “This only would I learn of you, Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?  
> **3** Are ye so foolish? having begun in the Spirit, are ye now made perfect by the flesh?  
> **5** He therefore that ministereth to you the Spirit, and worketh miracles among you, doeth he it by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?  
> **14** That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ; that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

**Context:** Paul appeals to the Galatians’ own experience: they received the Spirit through hearing the gospel with faith, not by performing works of the law. Christ redeemed His people so that Abraham’s blessing and the promised Spirit would come to Gentiles through faith.

---

### Galatians 4:6, 29

> **6** “And because ye are sons, God hath sent forth the Spirit of his Son into your hearts, crying, Abba, Father.  
> **29** But as then he that was born after the flesh persecuted him that was born after the Spirit, even so it is now.”

**Context:** God sends the **“Spirit of his Son”** into believers’ hearts, giving them the filial cry of Jesus and confirming that they are children rather than slaves. In Paul’s comparison of Hagar and Sarah, Isaac’s birth by divine promise is described as birth “after the Spirit.”

---

### Galatians 5:5, 16–18, 22, 25

> **5** “For we through the Spirit wait for the hope of righteousness by faith.  
> **16** This I say then, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.  
> **17** For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other: so that ye cannot do the things that ye would.  
> **18** But if ye be led of the Spirit, ye are not under the law.  
> **22** But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith,  
> **25** If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit.”

**Context:** Christian freedom is neither legalism nor permission to indulge sinful desire. The Spirit creates hope, opposes the flesh, leads believers, and produces godly character. Those whose life comes from the Spirit must keep in step with His direction.

---

### Galatians 6:8

> “For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting.”

**Context:** Paul applies the principle of sowing and reaping to moral life. Persistent self-indulgence ends in corruption, whereas investing one’s life in what the Spirit desires leads toward the harvest of eternal life.

## 5. Ephesians

### Ephesians 1:13, 17

> **13** “In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,  
> **17** That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him:”

**Context:** Those who hear and believe the gospel are sealed in Christ with the promised Holy Spirit, marking them as God’s people and guaranteeing their inheritance. Paul then prays for divine wisdom and revelation so that they may know God more fully. “The spirit of wisdom” may refer directly to the Holy Spirit as giver of revelation or to a Spirit-produced disposition of wisdom.

---

### Ephesians 2:18, 22

> **18** “For through him we both have access by one Spirit unto the Father.  
> **22** In whom ye also are builded together for an habitation of God through the Spirit.”

**Context:** Through Christ, Jewish and Gentile believers approach the one Father by the one Spirit. Together they are being formed into a holy Temple in which God dwells through His Spirit.

---

### Ephesians 3:5, 16

> **5** “Which in other ages was not made known unto the sons of men, as it is now revealed unto his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit;  
> **16** That he would grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man;”

**Context:** The Spirit revealed the previously hidden mystery that Gentiles are fellow heirs in Christ through the gospel. Paul prays that the same Spirit will strengthen believers inwardly so that Christ may dwell in their hearts through faith.

---

### Ephesians 4:3–4, 30

> **3** “Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.  
> **4** There is one body, and one Spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling;  
> **30** And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.”

**Context:** Believers do not manufacture Christian unity; they are charged to preserve the unity already created by the one Spirit. Their speech and conduct must not grieve the personal Holy Spirit who has sealed them for final redemption.

---

### Ephesians 5:18

> “And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

**Context:** Rather than surrendering control to drunkenness, believers are commanded to live continually under the Spirit’s filling. The following verses describe worship, thanksgiving, and mutual submission as results of this Spirit-governed life.

---

### Ephesians 6:17–18

> **17** “And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God:  
> **18** Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints;”

**Context:** In the armor-of-God passage, the word of God is the sword supplied and employed by the Spirit. Spiritual conflict also requires persistent prayer shaped and empowered by the Spirit for all God’s people.

## 6. Philippians

### Philippians 1:19

> “For I know that this shall turn to my salvation through your prayer, and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ,”

**Context:** Though imprisoned and opposed, Paul expected God to sustain and vindicate him through the Philippians’ prayers and the provision of the **“Spirit of Jesus Christ.”** This title emphasizes that the Holy Spirit communicates the presence and life of the risen Christ.

---

### Philippians 2:1

> “If there be therefore any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any bowels and mercies,”

**Context:** Paul appeals to blessings the Philippians already share as the basis for humility and unity. “Fellowship of the Spirit” can include both participation in the Spirit and the shared fellowship He creates among believers.

---

### Philippians 3:3

> “For we are the circumcision, which worship God in the spirit, and rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh.”

**Context:** Against teachers who placed confidence in physical circumcision, Paul identifies God’s true covenant people as those who worship by the Spirit, glory in Christ, and renounce confidence in human status or achievement. The KJV prints “spirit” in lowercase, while many modern translations explicitly read **“by the Spirit of God.”**

## 7. Colossians

### Colossians 1:8

> “Who also declared unto us your love in the Spirit.”

**Context:** Epaphras reported the Colossians’ faith in Christ and love for all the saints. Their love was not merely natural affection but love created and sustained in the sphere and power of the Spirit.

## 8. First Thessalonians

### 1 Thessalonians 1:5–6

> **5** “For our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance; as ye know what manner of men we were among you for your sake.

**Context:** The Thessalonians’ conversion displayed God’s choice because the gospel came with Spirit-given power and conviction. The same Holy Ghost gave them joy as they received the message despite severe affliction.

### 1 Thessalonians 4:8

> “He therefore that despiseth, despiseth not man, but God, who hath also given unto us his holy Spirit.”

---

### 1 Thessalonians 5:19

> “Quench not the Spirit.”

**Context:** Among short instructions for congregational life, Paul warns believers not to suppress the Spirit’s activity. The next verses require them neither to despise prophecy indiscriminately nor accept everything uncritically, but to test all things and retain what is good.

## 9. Second Thessalonians

### 2 Thessalonians 2:13

> “But we are bound to give thanks alway to God for you, brethren beloved of the Lord, because God hath from the beginning chosen you to salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth:”

**Context:** In contrast with those who embrace deception, believers are saved through the Spirit’s sanctifying work joined with faith in the truth. Paul therefore gives thanks for God’s gracious initiative in their salvation.

## 10. First Timothy

### 1 Timothy 4:1

> “Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils;”

**Context:** The Holy Spirit clearly warned of future apostasy and deceptive teaching. Paul contrasts the singular divine Spirit who speaks truth with the evil spirits that promote doctrines opposed to the gospel.

## 11. Second Timothy

### 2 Timothy 1:14

> “That good thing which was committed unto thee keep by the Holy Ghost which dwelleth in us.”

**Context:** Timothy was entrusted with the apostolic gospel and charged to guard it faithfully. This responsibility could be fulfilled not by human strength alone but through the indwelling Holy Ghost.

## 12. Titus

### Titus 3:5–6

> **5** “Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;  
> **6** Which he shed on us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Saviour;”

**Context:** Salvation rests on God’s mercy rather than human merit. The Holy Ghost brings regeneration and renewal and is poured out abundantly through Jesus Christ, preparing believers for justification and the hope of eternal life.

## 13. Philemon

Philemon contains **no direct reference to the Holy Spirit**. Paul nevertheless emphasizes Spirit-consistent realities such as faith, love, fellowship, reconciliation, and receiving Onesimus as a beloved brother in Christ.

## Part 4 — Hebrews and the General Epistles

## 1. Hebrews

### Hebrews 2:4

> “God also bearing them witness, both with signs and wonders, and with divers miracles, and gifts of the Holy Ghost, according to his own will?”

**Context:** The readers are warned not to neglect the salvation first announced by the Lord and confirmed by eyewitnesses. God authenticated that apostolic witness through miracles and distributions of the Holy Ghost according to His sovereign will.

---

### Hebrews 3:7

> “Wherefore as the Holy Ghost saith, To day if ye will hear his voice,”

**Context:** Hebrews introduces Psalm 95 as the present speech of the Holy Ghost. The ancient warning about Israel’s hardened heart still addresses the church “today,” calling believers to hear and persevere.

---

### Hebrews 6:4

> “For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Holy Ghost,”

**Context:** This begins one of Hebrews’ strongest warnings against apostasy. The people described had profoundly experienced the blessings of the Christian community, including participation in the Holy Ghost; the passage urges perseverance rather than speculation about how little one may experience and still turn away.

---

### Hebrews 9:8, 14

> **8** “The Holy Ghost this signifying, that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest, while as the first tabernacle was yet standing:  
> **14** How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

**Context:** The Spirit signified through the tabernacle’s restricted access that the old arrangement did not yet provide the final opened way into God’s presence. Christ, however, offered Himself through the **eternal Spirit**, and His blood cleanses the conscience for living service to God.

---

### Hebrews 10:15, 29

> **15** “Whereof the Holy Ghost also is a witness to us: for after that he had said before,  
> **29** Of how much sorer punishment, suppose ye, shall he be thought worthy, who hath trodden under foot the Son of God, and hath counted the blood of the covenant, wherewith he was sanctified, an unholy thing, and hath done despite unto the Spirit of grace?”

**Context:** The Holy Ghost bears present witness through Jeremiah’s promise of a new covenant in which God writes His law upon hearts and remembers sin no more. To reject the Son and covenant blood after receiving such knowledge is also to insult the **Spirit of grace**.

### Special titles in Hebrews

- **Eternal Spirit** — Hebrews 9:14 emphasizes the Spirit’s relation to Christ’s once-for-all self-offering.
- **Spirit of grace** — Hebrews 10:29 portrays deliberate apostasy as contempt for the gracious Spirit who applies new-covenant blessing.

---

## 2. James

### James 4:5 — A Disputed Reference

> “Do ye think that the scripture saith in vain, The spirit that dwelleth in us lusteth to envy?”

**Context:** James rebukes friendship with the world and calls his readers to undivided loyalty to God. The difficult Greek wording has several possible senses. The “spirit” may be the human spirit God caused to dwell in humanity and its envious desires, or it may refer to God’s Spirit jealously desiring His people’s faithfulness. The KJV’s lowercase wording should therefore not be treated as an unambiguous Holy Spirit title.

---

## 3. First Peter

### 1 Peter 1:2, 11–12

> **2** “Elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ: Grace unto you, and peace, be multiplied.  
> **11** Searching what, or what manner of time the Spirit of Christ which was in them did signify, when it testified beforehand the sufferings of Christ, and the glory that should follow.  
> **12** Unto whom it was revealed, that not unto themselves, but unto us they did minister the things, which are now reported unto you by them that have preached the gospel unto you with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven; which things the angels desire to look into.”

**Context:** Peter describes salvation in relation to the Father’s foreknowledge, the Spirit’s sanctification, and Jesus Christ’s blood. The **Spirit of Christ** spoke beforehand through the prophets concerning Christ’s suffering and glory, and the same Holy Ghost empowered those who later preached the fulfilled gospel.

---

### 1 Peter 4:14

> “If ye be reproached for the name of Christ, happy are ye; for the spirit of glory and of God resteth upon you: on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified.”

**Context:** Christians who suffer specifically for bearing Christ’s name are not abandoned or disgraced before God. The **Spirit of glory and of God** rests upon them, echoing the Spirit’s resting upon the Messiah in Isaiah and assuring them of divine favor.

### Possible reference in 1 Peter 3:18

> “For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh, but quickened by the Spirit:”

This may mean that the Holy Spirit made Christ alive, which is how the KJV’s capitalization can be read. Many interpreters instead understand a contrast between the sphere of Christ’s bodily death—“in the flesh”—and His resurrection life—“in spirit.” It is therefore a possible rather than certain direct reference.

---

## 4. Second Peter

### 2 Peter 1:21

> “For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.”

**Context:** Prophecy did not originate in a prophet’s independent impulse or private interpretation. The Holy Ghost carried the human speakers along, so their genuinely human words communicated God’s message.

---

## 5. First John

### 1 John 3:24

> “And he that keepeth his commandments dwelleth in him, and he in him. And hereby we know that he abideth in us, by the Spirit which he hath given us.”

**Context:** Obedient faith and love mark mutual fellowship between God and believers. The gift of the Spirit provides inward assurance that God truly abides in His people.

---

### 1 John 4:2, 6, 13

> **2** “Hereby know ye the Spirit of God: Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God:  
> **6** We are of God: he that knoweth God heareth us; he that is not of God heareth not us. Hereby know we the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error.  
> **13** Hereby know we that we dwell in him, and he in us, because he hath given us of his Spirit.”

**Context:** Claims of spiritual inspiration must be tested by their confession of the incarnate Jesus and their reception of apostolic truth. The Spirit of God bears true witness to Christ, while the “spirit of error” animates false teaching. God’s gift of His Spirit also assures believers of abiding fellowship with Him.

---

### 1 John 5:6–8

> **6** “This is he that came by water and blood, even Jesus Christ; not by water only, but by water and blood. And it is the Spirit that beareth witness, because the Spirit is truth.  
> **7** For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one.  
> **8** And there are three that bear witness in earth, the spirit, and the water, and the blood: and these three agree in one.”

**Context:** John presents converging testimony that Jesus is the Son of God: the water, the blood, and the truth-bearing Spirit. This witness grounds the believer’s confidence in God’s testimony concerning His Son.

---

## 7. Jude

### Jude 19–20

> **19** “These be they who separate themselves, sensual, having not the Spirit.  
> **20** But ye, beloved, building up yourselves on your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Ghost,”

**Context:** Jude contrasts divisive false teachers, who lack the Spirit, with faithful believers. Christians resist deception by strengthening one another in the apostolic faith and praying **“in the Holy Ghost”**—prayer shaped, enabled, and directed by the Spirit.

## Part 5 — The Spirit in Revelation

The exact title **“Holy Spirit”** or **“Holy Ghost”** does not appear in Revelation. The book nevertheless repeatedly speaks of **“the Spirit,” “the seven Spirits of God,”** and John being **“in the Spirit.”** Because Revelation communicates through symbolic visions, some expressions require careful interpretation.

## 1. The Spirit Speaking to the Churches

### Revelation 2:7

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God.”

**Context:** The risen Christ calls the church at Ephesus to recover its first love. The Spirit applies Christ’s message to all the churches and promises final life in God’s paradise to the one who overcomes.

---

### Revelation 2:11

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death.”

**Context:** The persecuted church at Smyrna is called to faithfulness even unto death. The Spirit assures faithful believers that human death cannot expose them to the final judgment called the second death.

---

### Revelation 2:17

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna, and will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it.”

**Context:** The church at Pergamos faced pressure from false teaching and idolatrous compromise. The Spirit promises hidden manna and a white stone with a new name—images of divine provision, acceptance, and belonging—to those who remain faithful.

---

### Revelation 2:29

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.”

**Context:** After warning Thyatira about tolerating corrupt teaching, Christ promises authority and the morning star to those who hold fast. The repeated refrain makes this message the Spirit’s word to every church, not merely one ancient congregation.

---

### Revelation 3:6

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.”

**Context:** Sardis had a reputation for life while being spiritually dead. The Spirit calls all churches to wakefulness, repentance, and the persevering faith represented by undefiled garments.

---

### Revelation 3:13

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.”

**Context:** Philadelphia had little strength yet had kept Christ’s word. The Spirit directs every church to hear the promises of preservation, vindication, and permanent belonging in God’s dwelling.

---

### Revelation 3:22

> “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.”

**Context:** Laodicea’s self-satisfaction concealed spiritual poverty and lukewarmness. The final refrain summons all churches to receive Christ’s correction, repent, and overcome.

### The repeated refrain

All seven messages contain the instruction **“He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.”** Although each message begins as the risen Christ’s words to a particular church, each ends as the Spirit’s present address to all the churches. Christ’s speech and the Spirit’s speech are thus presented in complete harmony.

## 2. The Seven Spirits of God

### Revelation 1:4

> “John to the seven churches which are in Asia: Grace be unto you, and peace, from him which is, and which was, and which is to come; and from the seven Spirits which are before his throne;”

**Context:** John’s greeting invokes grace and peace from the eternal One, the seven Spirits before His throne, and Jesus Christ. The placement contributes to the common interpretation that this is symbolic language for the fullness of the Holy Spirit.

---

### Revelation 3:1

> “And unto the angel of the church in Sardis write; These things saith he that hath the seven Spirits of God, and the seven stars; I know thy works, that thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead.”

**Context:** Christ possesses the seven Spirits and the seven stars. His fullness of spiritual life exposes Sardis’s deadness and makes Him able to awaken and restore the church.

---

### Revelation 4:5

> “And out of the throne proceeded lightnings and thunderings and voices: and there were seven lamps of fire burning before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God.”

**Context:** In John’s vision of the heavenly throne room, seven burning lamps represent the seven Spirits of God. The imagery associates them with God’s holy, searching, and active presence before the throne.

---

### Revelation 5:6

> “And I beheld, and, lo, in the midst of the throne and of the four beasts, and in the midst of the elders, stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth.”

**Context:** The slain yet standing Lamb possesses perfect power, represented by seven horns, and perfect sight, represented by seven eyes. The seven Spirits are sent throughout the earth, linking the Lamb’s universal rule with God’s all-seeing spiritual activity.

---

## 3. John “in the Spirit”

### Revelation 1:10

> “I was in the Spirit on the Lord’s day, and heard behind me a great voice, as of a trumpet,”

**Context:** John was exiled on Patmos when the Spirit brought him into a prophetic state in which he heard the risen Christ and received the messages for the seven churches.

---

### Revelation 4:2

> “And immediately I was in the spirit: and, behold, a throne was set in heaven, and one sat on the throne.”

**Context:** After a door opened in heaven and a voice summoned John upward, he was immediately “in the spirit” and saw God’s throne. The phrase marks a new stage of Spirit-enabled revelation.

---

### Revelation 17:3

> “So he carried me away in the spirit into the wilderness: and I saw a woman sit upon a scarlet coloured beast, full of names of blasphemy, having seven heads and ten horns.”

**Context:** An angel carried John in the spirit to the wilderness to see symbolic judgment upon Babylon, portrayed as a corrupt woman allied with a blasphemous beast.

---

### Revelation 21:10

> “And he carried me away in the spirit to a great and high mountain, and shewed me that great city, the holy Jerusalem, descending out of heaven from God,”

**Context:** In deliberate contrast with the wilderness vision of corrupt Babylon, John is carried in the spirit to a high mountain to see the holy Jerusalem, the perfected people and dwelling of God.

### Meaning of the expression

These passages describe John entering a prophetic or visionary state through the Spirit. The expression does not mean that the visions were mere products of his imagination; Revelation presents them as divinely given disclosures communicated through symbolic scenes.

## 4. Other References

### Revelation 14:13

> “And I heard a voice from heaven saying unto me, Write, Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them.”

**Context:** Amid warnings about worshiping the beast and a call for the saints’ endurance, the Spirit confirms the blessedness of those who die faithful to the Lord. Their labor ends in rest, but the fruit of their faithfulness is not lost.

---

### Revelation 19:10

> “And I fell at his feet to worship him. And he said unto me, See thou do it not: I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren that have the testimony of Jesus: worship God: for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.”

**Context:** The angel refuses John’s worship and directs it to God alone. “The spirit of prophecy” may mean that testimony concerning Jesus is the heart and animating purpose of true prophecy; because the phrase is not an explicit personal title, it should not automatically be equated with a direct reference to the Holy Spirit.

---

### Revelation 22:17

> “And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.”

**Context:** At the close of Scripture, the Spirit and the church together say, “Come.” The appeal can be heard both as longing for Christ’s return and as an invitation for thirsty people to receive the water of life freely.

---

### Revelation 11:11 — Spirit or Breath of Life

> “And after three days and an half the Spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood upon their feet; and great fear fell upon them which saw them.”

**Context:** God raises the two witnesses after their enemies have killed them. The KJV has **“the Spirit of life from God,”** while many translations render the expression **“breath of life from God.”** The imagery recalls God giving life to Adam and Ezekiel’s dry bones; it describes God’s life-giving power but is not necessarily a direct title for the Holy Spirit.
